WHY

Great question, why did I create this weird little satire site. Now, as one may have picked up, I am not particularly a fan of generative AI, matter of fact I'd consider myself to be something of the opposite. But why? Why do I stand in the face of what appears at first to be the next big step for humanity. We've mad a robot that doesn't only talk, it talks new things! Let's take it step by step and discuss why GPT artificial intelligence is a net loss for humanity.

1. The Environment. I am not a hippie, I might verge on hippie occasionally, and my hair is getting pretty long, but I wouldn't consider myself a staunch environmentalist, I have yet to tie myself to a tree to make a swing, nevermind to stand in the way of logging. Arificial intelligence is a major drain on the environment however. Data centres are large, like, huge, often tens or hundreds of acres large in total, and they are hungry boys, or girls. The average AI data centre takes up the energy requirements of 100,000 homes (IEA Energy and AI -Executive summary). In a world already trying hard to control emissions from fossil fuels, another 100,000 homes appearing often on the grid will surely allow for more power to be sustainably generated, right? Think about Nemo for a second, too. Data centres are thirsty, and large ones can consume the requirements of a town of 50,000, daily (Environmental and Energy Study Instiute - Data Centers and Water Consumption). In a world where human beings don't have enough to drink in some areas, why are we feeding our thirst for AI generated instagram reels before we feed our thirst for water? Not only do data centres consume products of the Earth, and dirty energy, many literally house the same dirty energy they use. In Memphis, xAI's datacentre has been sued by an African American rights association for violating environmental laws in an area predominantly inhabited by African Americans, pumping out 1700 tons of harmful gasses yearly. (The Guardian - NAACP lawsuit accuses Elon Musk’s xAI of polluting Black neighborhoods near Memphis)

2. I am sure I have finished my discussion on the environmental impact, so let's look at how it effects the people who live nearby personnally. Datacentres are noisy, really noisy, 85-100 decibels noisy (World Resources Institute - US Data Centre Growth Impacts), with thanks to their polluting generators. Datacentres are often built near residential areas, and if you dislike the sound of a blender for the minutes they work, you best believe you're unlikely to like the sound of a constant generator, humming in your ear constantly. Back to the environment too, imagine a bird cannot hear the mating call of another in a "protected" area, due to the overwhelming noise of you, generating a picture of your best friend in a funny costume, or your cat in a funny hat.

3. Lastly, I have no doubt someone will still come to say, "well, I don't live near any datacentres, and I don't care an inch for Nemo, or any of his buddies in the natural world, I think it's all well and fine to continue as we are. I see obviously you must be extremely practical, as am I. Let's see how it effects everyone, in the world. First and foremost, you start paying more. Unless you a hermit, and live off the grid, you will pay more for energy. It is no longer the 19th Century, and electricity grids are not city, but nationwide, and when something takes more power, it comes into your bill. The economy too, will suffer, though it may seem more money is changing hands, it changes between the top 1% of the top 1%. The layman, laid off from his job as a creative, a writer or a coder (hits close to home, perhaps), earns less money. His local grocer, already struggling under the boot of the large chain, has one less customer willing to pay the premium for local. Whatever business you work in, unless it's AI, will suffer, and less money actually, really rotating in the economy, is a bad thing. Trust me.

This site really, is just for fun. You can't make real change by writing a few paragraphs in a sarcastic tone online, not really. You CAN make change by going out, talking to people. Interact with the people who continually use AI, let them know what I've just told you. Go out, seek out protests to make your voice heard, sign petitions. If there aren't protests nor petitions, go make some, stop NGPTing stupid, unncessary questions, and go make your impact. It may seem an entirely unwinnable fight, but by working together, I am relatively assured by my own personal chatgpt, that we might just stand a chance. Or maybe that's just what Grok told me, anyway.
